## Digital Experience Notes

### Homepage
- Cleaner than most peers in its tier. Uses a hierarchical mega-menu and well-organized trust section.
- Trust badges prominently displayed: Great Place to Work, ISO, ESOMAR, D-U-N-S certification. Comparatively heavy on credentialing.
- Testimonial carousel includes downloadable letters of recommendation — unusually substantive social proof.
- CTA buttons for "Download Sample" and "Analyst Consultation" are well-placed.
- Strategic use of icons to communicate cost-efficiency story (distributed expertise, location advantage, fast delivery).
- Branding is clean but generic — navy/blue palette similar to ~90% of the category.

### Report Catalog / Store UX
- 26,683+ reports organized through deep nested industry taxonomy.
- 19+ industries with multiple sub-levels of categorization.
- "TRENDING REPORTS" section highlights popular analyses with CAGR and study period metadata.
- No faceted filtering (price, date, region, etc.) visible in the catalog.
- No pricing visible on report cards — requires clicking into individual report pages.

### Individual Report Page UX
- Expandable, detailed TOC is prominently featured mid-page — one of the better implementations in the category.
- Multiple chart previews embedded directly on the page (market size, segment share, regional CAGR comparisons).
- Revenue projections with specific figures shown (e.g., USD 434.42B → USD 2,503.13B for AI market).
- Mordor Intelligence watermark visible on preview charts — protects IP while previewing.
- Sample download modal: lightweight form (name, business email, phone). Copy emphasizes security ("Your data is secure and never sold or shared").
- "Buy Now" button present but no upfront pricing; "Check Report Pricing" appears after sample request.
- Multiple CTAs: "Download Sample," "Buy Now," "Customize Now," "Get Detailed Market Forecasts."
- Author attribution and publication dates reinforce freshness.

### Checkout / Lead-Capture Flow
- Multi-step: browse → view report → download sample (form fill) → analyst contact → pricing → purchase.
- No self-service e-commerce checkout visible.
- Lead capture is aggressive but not unusually so for the category.

### Dashboards / Platform
- **Synapse** subscription product referenced but no public demo of the interface.
- **Data Center Intelligence** database product — niche offering for colocation/data center segment.
- [Unverified] Synapse is assumed to be a report-access portal with basic search; no evidence of interactive dashboards.

### Mobile Responsiveness
- Appears responsive; mega-menus likely collapse to hamburger nav on mobile.
- No dedicated app found.

### Loading Speed (subjective)
- Fast to moderate. Fewer heavy assets on homepage than some peers.

### Search / Discoverability
- Search bar present on homepage; category navigation is the primary discovery mechanism.
- No AI-assisted search or recommendation engine visible.
- Trending reports section aids serendipitous discovery.

## Standout Strength

- **Individual report page quality** is above the Tier A average — embedded charts, detailed TOC, and specific data points in previews give buyers more confidence before purchasing than most peers offer.
- **Self-funded growth** from $0 to estimated $187M (if accurate) signals strong product-market fit with core customers.
- **Synapse subscription platform** gives them a path toward recurring revenue that most pure-play peers lack.
- **ESOMAR and ISO credentialing** — methodological legitimacy markers that matter to enterprise buyers.

---

## Visible Weakness

- **No pricing transparency.** Fully opaque in an era where buyers expect at least a range.
- **Catalog navigation is taxonomy-heavy** — 26,000+ reports are only navigable through category drilling; no power search.
- **Generic visual identity.** The site is functional but indistinguishable from 10 other Indian research firms.
- **Mobile experience** on deep catalog pages is likely poor given the taxonomy depth.
- **Revenue estimate discrepancy** (self-funded from Hyderabad at $187M feels aggressive; [unverified]).

---

## What Ken Research Should Steal / Avoid

- **Steal:** The practice of embedding actual chart previews and specific data points on individual report pages. This reduces buyer risk and increases conversion without giving away the report.
- **Steal:** Downloadable client recommendation letters as trust proof — more credible than a logo carousel.
- **Avoid:** Taxonomy-only navigation. At 26,000+ reports, Mordor's catalog is effectively unsearchable without AI-assisted discovery.
