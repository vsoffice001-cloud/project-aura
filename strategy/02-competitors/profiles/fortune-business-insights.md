## Digital Experience Notes

### Homepage
- Multi-level navigation with industry dropdown menus.
- Prominent client testimonial carousel featuring logos from Reuters, IBM, Forbes, CNBC — media logos alongside corporate logos is an interesting credibility mix.
- Case study cards highlighting specific consulting outcomes — tangible proof of advisory work.
- Tabbed "Trending Reports by Industries" section — useful serendipitous discovery feature.
- Integrated blog and press release feeds — content marketing is active.
- Contact forms and quote request CTAs throughout.
- **Visual design:** professional and cleaner than many Tier A peers; uses whitespace more effectively than most; still clearly template-based but the template is a better one.

### Report Catalog / Store UX
- Industry-based navigation.
- Price shown on listing cards ($4,850 range) — this is a significant differentiator.
- "Trending Reports" tabbed section aids discovery beyond pure navigation.
- No faceted filtering observed.

### Individual Report Page UX
- **Full inquiry/conversion funnel** embedded on each report page.
- TOC available but hidden behind a tab (click to expand) — requires user action to see structure.
- "Download Free Sample" CTA appears at minimum three times on a page.
- Sample download: multi-step gate with name, email, security verification.
- Multiple form instances throughout the page for lead nurturing.
- Final lead-capture modal: "Unlock [Market] Intelligence" with contact fields — aggressive but standard for the category.
- Trust signals: client logos (Toyota, Microsoft, Samsung), ESOMAR/ISO certifications, Comodo Secure, DMCA protection, "Great Place to Work" badge.
- "Buy Now" button prominent but no upfront price on the report page itself (price shown in catalog but not always on individual page — inconsistency noted).
- CTAs: "Download Free Sample," "Speak to Analyst," "Customize Now," "Buy Now," "Get Quote," "Request Consultation" — at least 6 distinct CTAs creates decision paralysis rather than clarity.

### Checkout / Lead-Capture Flow
- Heavy lead-capture infrastructure.
- Multiple form instances = aggressive lead capture.
- No self-service e-commerce checkout.
- CTA proliferation creates cognitive overload; too many options reduce conversion intent.

### Dashboards / Platform
- No data platform or interactive dashboard visible.
- Pure report + consulting model.

### Mobile Responsiveness
- Appears responsive; multi-level navigation likely has mobile UX challenges.
- No dedicated mobile app.

### Loading Speed (subjective)
- Moderate. Multiple embedded forms and scripts slow page loads.

### Search / Discoverability
- Search bar present but not prominently featured.
- Navigation and "Trending Reports" are the primary discovery aids.
- Blog/press releases provide SEO entry points.

## Standout Strength

- **Partial pricing transparency** — listing per-report prices on catalog pages ($4,850) is more buyer-friendly than inquiry-only peers.
- **Consulting proof points** — case study cards showing specific advisory outcomes give the firm more credibility as a consulting partner, not just a report publisher.
- **Content marketing** — active blog and press releases generate SEO traffic and demonstrate ongoing topical expertise.
- **Media brand logos** (Reuters, IBM, Forbes) alongside client logos is an unusual and effective trust signal combination.

---

## Visible Weakness

- **CTA proliferation** — six or more distinct CTAs on a single report page is a known conversion killer. Decision paralysis is real.
- **Aggressive modal lead capture** — the "Unlock [Market] Intelligence" modal is a friction point at the wrong moment in the buyer journey.
- **No data platform or dashboard.**
- **Inconsistency between catalog pricing and individual report page pricing** — price shown in catalog may not appear on the report page itself.
- **Employee count and revenue opacity** makes competitive positioning harder to assess.

---

## What Ken Research Should Steal / Avoid

- **Steal:** Showing price on the catalog listing card (even a starting price). This is the right practice for reducing buyer anxiety before they click.
- **Steal:** Case study cards that show specific consulting outcomes — not just testimonials, but demonstrated results.
- **Avoid:** The modal lead-capture trigger at the bottom of report pages. It is industry-standard but genuinely alienates buyers. Ken Research could differentiate by having a cleaner, less aggressive lead-capture model.
- **Avoid:** More than 3 distinct CTAs on any single page. Consolidate the decision.
