## One-Line Positioning

**In their own words:** "The World's Largest Market Research Store"  
**Plain language:** Dublin-based aggregator and publisher of market research reports — essentially a marketplace model layered with some proprietary research production. Competes on sheer catalog volume and brand recognition.

## Pricing Visible on Site

**Yes — partially.** Individual report pages show clear pricing. Example seen: single-user €838 / USD $950; enterprise €1,279 / USD $1,450 with currency toggle. Multi-currency support (USD, EUR, GBP) is visible in the header. This is meaningfully more transparent than most Tier A peers.

## Digital Experience Notes

### Homepage
- Functional but visually dated — heavy reliance on extensive navigation menus.
- Client logo carousel featuring 50+ brand logos.
- Media mention logos (Time, Reuters, CNN) — social proof from media rather than just clients; unusual in the category.
- Google Reviews badge: 4.8/5 stars from 282 reviews — one of few in the category to surface Google reviews on-site.
- Multiple regional phone numbers in the header: practical for global buyers, but creates visual clutter.
- Multi-currency selector in the header is a useful commerce-first detail.
- Overall aesthetic: early-2010s enterprise web. Dense, text-heavy, not visually differentiated.

### Report Catalog / Store UX
- Very large catalog with hierarchical category browsing as the primary navigation.
- Filtering options visible on category pages (though not as sophisticated as modern e-commerce platforms).
- Report cards include title, brief description, and sometimes pricing.
- The "store" framing is more explicit here than at peers — shopping cart / basket model.

### Individual Report Page UX
- **Pricing displayed clearly on the page** — one of the best in class for price transparency.
- Two-tier pricing (single user vs. enterprise) shown side-by-side.
- "Add to Basket" purchase flow — genuine e-commerce rather than inquiry model.
- "Request Quote" option available for customization.
- Sample download form: name, email, job title, organization, country — moderate friction.
- Sample copy framed as "to help you make an informed purchasing decision."
- TOC visibility: minimal — limited chapter descriptions, not full expandable TOC.
- Ask-a-question form, callback request, customization inquiry — multiple conversion paths.
- Report description quality varies widely (depends on publisher).
- Page layout: vertical funnel. Clear product title → description → pricing → sample → purchase.

### Checkout / Lead-Capture Flow
- **Most e-commerce-like in Tier A** — actual "Add to Basket" functionality.
- Multiple purchase-trigger points on each report page.
- Phone contact prominently displayed for high-friction buyers.

### Dashboards / Platform
- No data platform or dashboard product visible.
- Pure report-store model.

### Mobile Responsiveness
- Appears to render on mobile; dense navigation menus will be problematic on small screens.
- No dedicated mobile app.

### Loading Speed (subjective)
- Moderate. Standard shared hosting feel. Some pages load slowly.

### Search / Discoverability
- Search bar present; basic keyword search.
- No AI-powered search or smart recommendations visible.
- Category-based browsing is primary discovery mechanism.
- Third-party reports from multiple publishers means content quality is inconsistent.

## Standout Strength

- **Pricing transparency** — showing actual prices on report pages with a shopping cart model is the most buyer-friendly approach in Tier A.
- **Scale and brand recognition** — "World's Largest Market Research Store" claim has credibility with buyers who want breadth over depth.
- **Multi-currency, multi-region** — practical for international enterprise buyers.
- **Google Reviews on-site** — one of the few in the category using third-party review platforms for social proof.
- **Established since 2002** — longevity as a trust signal in B2B.

---

## Visible Weakness

- **Dated visual design** — the website looks significantly older than most competitors. High information density with low visual hierarchy.
- **Aggregator model weakness** — quality varies widely across publishers; no consistent methodology or voice.
- **Thin TOC previews** — buyers can't assess report quality before purchasing; the sample request is the only preview mechanism.
- **No platform/dashboard product** — entirely static report delivery.
- **Homepage navigation is overwhelming** — too many categories, too many phone numbers, too many logos.

---

## What Ken Research Should Steal / Avoid

- **Steal:** Clear upfront pricing on report pages with an "Add to Cart" option. This is table stakes in 2026 and Research and Markets is ahead of most peers here.
- **Steal:** Multi-currency display in the header — a small UX detail that matters enormously for international buyers.
- **Avoid:** The aggregator model that creates inconsistent quality. Ken Research should lean into consistent methodology and voice as a differentiator from the "marketplace" approach.
