## Standout Strength

- **Frost Radar™ as a proprietary framework** — creates a flywheel where vendors pay to be benchmarked and promote the results, generating revenue AND marketing the firm simultaneously. Similar to Gartner Magic Quadrant.
- **60+ years of brand equity** — Frost & Sullivan is institutionally recognized by enterprise strategy and innovation teams globally.
- **Events business (Executive MindXchange)** — in-person executive access is a relationship moat that digital-only players can't replicate.
- **"Growth Pipeline as a Service" positioning** — frames research as a continuous, outcome-linked service rather than a transactional purchase.
- **Baymard UX benchmark participation** — investing in formal UX benchmarking signals design maturity awareness.

---

## Visible Weakness

- **Website 403 errors** during research suggest either blocking or availability issues — significant for an enterprise-facing brand.
- **The awards/recognition business model** has faced industry skepticism — "pay-to-be-on-the-radar" mechanics can erode research credibility.
- **Revenue estimate** (~$253.6M) suggests scale pressure given 1,200–1,800 employee headcount.
- **Unclear how the advisory and report store experiences connect** — two separate URLs (frost.com + store.frost.com) may create fragmented buyer journeys.

---

## What Ken Research Should Steal / Avoid

- **Steal:** A proprietary benchmarking framework that has marketing value for the companies included — a Frost Radar / Magic Quadrant equivalent. Ken Research could build a "Ken Index" for specific verticals.
- **Steal:** Events as relationship-building and research promotion — Executive Summits or online roundtables turn research into a community, not just a product.
- **Avoid:** The fragmented digital estate (advisory site + separate store) — a unified experience is better for buyer journeys.
