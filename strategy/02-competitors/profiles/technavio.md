## Digital Experience Notes

### Homepage
- Hierarchical mega-menu navigation with nested industry categories.
- Central search functionality prominent in header — above average emphasis on search-first vs. browse-first.
- Email signup for promotions — unusual in B2B research; suggests a more mass-market positioning.
- Footer contains blog, press releases, webinars, glossary — reasonable content depth.
- Social media links (LinkedIn, X/Twitter) in footer.
- Multi-region contact information (US and UK phone numbers).
- Copyright notice: "Copyright © 2007-2026 Infiniti Research Limited."
- **Overall design aesthetic: this is the most dated-looking site in Tier A.** The design language reads as 2010-era web — heavy borders, dated icon sets, functional but visually unappealing.

### Report Catalog / Store UX
- GICS-aligned vertical navigation is actually well-structured conceptually.
- 100+ subsectors gives good granularity.
- Filtering and search available but limited in sophistication.
- Report cards: basic title/description; no pricing visible.

### Individual Report Page UX
- **Critical finding: the specific AI market report tested was listed as "being upgraded."** This signals active catalog maintenance but also that some catalog entries are non-functional at any given time.
- For active report pages: standard inquiry form (Name, Business Email, Phone, Message).
- reCAPTCHA protection present.
- No pricing, TOC, or chart previews observable on the tested (upgrading) page.
- Based on category patterns: likely standard TOC tab, sample request, inquiry CTA.

### Checkout / Lead-Capture Flow
- Inquiry-first model.
- Basic contact form with reCAPTCHA.
- Subscription services CTAs exist but no self-service signup visible.

### Dashboards / Platform
- No data platform or interactive dashboard product visible.
- Subscription appears to be a report-access portal rather than an analytics product.

### Mobile Responsiveness
- Appears to have responsive elements; deep navigation likely problematic on mobile.
- No dedicated mobile app.

### Loading Speed (subjective)
- Slow to moderate. Older site architecture feel.

### Search / Discoverability
- Search bar in header — the most prominent search placement in Tier A.
- No AI search or smart filtering observed.
- Glossary section in footer is a useful SEO and educational resource, uncommon in the category.

## Standout Strength

- **Search bar prominence** — placing search front-and-center in the header is a more user-friendly discovery model than most Tier A peers who lead with mega-menu navigation.
- **GICS-aligned sector taxonomy** — aligning categories to a standard financial sector classification makes it easier for professional buyers (analysts, investors) to find relevant research.
- **Longevity** — operating since ~2004 gives Technavio strong SEO domain authority and institutional recognition.
- **Glossary / educational content** — a rare content asset in the category that builds discoverability and trust.

---

## Visible Weakness

- **Most visually dated site in Tier A.** The design looks like it was last significantly updated in 2012. Trust erosion risk for premium enterprise buyers.
- **Report pages actively under upgrade** — finding a key report (AI market) in "upgrade" state is a catalog management failure visible to prospective customers.
- **No pricing transparency.**
- **No interactive platform or dashboard.**
- **Email signup for promotions** suggests a mass-market orientation that may not align with the firm's enterprise positioning.
- **Employee count and revenue are entirely opaque** — even relative to peers who share some figures.

---

## What Ken Research Should Steal / Avoid

- **Steal:** Header-level search prominence — it's the right UX pattern for a large catalog. Ken Research should consider a search-first entry point over browse-first.
- **Steal:** Glossary section — glossaries drive SEO and establish topical authority without requiring expensive content production.
- **Avoid:** The dated visual aesthetic entirely. Technavio is the clearest example in the category of what "not investing in design" looks like over time. The functional content may be good, but the site communicates low trustworthiness to a first-time visitor.
