# Digital Experience Scorecard — Market Research Competitive Set

**Scope:** 20 competitors across 3 tiers  
**Primary lens:** UI, UX, and design experience — not research content quality  
**Last updated:** April 2026  
**Scoring method:** 1–5 scale per dimension; higher = better, EXCEPT for Lead-Capture Friction where lower = better  

---

## Scoring Dimensions

| Dimension | What It Measures |
|---|---|
| **Visual Design Quality** | Typography, color, imagery, layout, overall aesthetic polish |
| **IA / Navigation** | Information architecture clarity, discoverability, menu logic |
| **Search** | Search prominence, quality, AI/NL capability |
| **Sample Preview UX** | Quality of report preview before purchase/lead-capture |
| **Mobile** | Responsiveness, usability on mobile screens |
| **Page Speed Feel** | Subjective load speed and responsiveness |
| **Trust / Credibility Cues** | Certifications, reviews, client logos, methodology transparency |
| **Lead-Capture Friction** | 1 = low friction (best), 5 = high friction (worst) |
| **Dashboard Quality** | Interactive data/platform quality (N/A if none) |

**Verdict:** As a platform product, Statista's dashboard offering demonstrates what happens when a research firm builds its product with consumer UX standards from the start. The embeddable chart format has created a passive distribution flywheel — Statista charts appear in millions of academic, journalistic, and corporate documents globally, generating brand recognition that no advertising budget could replicate. The API and MCP integrations signal forward-thinking developer UX. The accessibility gap (charts not screen-reader compatible) and the width-over-depth data model are genuine limitations. But the combination of transparent pricing, free tier, search-first UX, and embeddable output format makes Statista's platform approach the most directly transferable model for Ken Research to study.

## Category-Wide UX Observation

The single most consistent failure across all 20 competitors is **the absence of a self-serve buyer evaluation path**. No Tier A competitor (except Statista and partially Research and Markets) allows a buyer to:

1. Find a relevant report through search
2. Preview meaningful content (not just a table of contents, but actual data)
3. See pricing
4. Purchase without speaking to anyone

This is not because research products can't be sold this way. Statista, Crunchbase, PitchBook, and CB Insights all demonstrate that research and intelligence products can be successfully sold through self-serve paths. It is because most Tier A firms have optimized for lead volume over buyer experience — a 2012-era B2B growth strategy that works until a better UX competitor enters the market.

The entire Tier A category is vulnerable to a competitor that simply builds a transparent, search-first, self-serve, price-visible research experience. That is the white space. That is the opportunity.
