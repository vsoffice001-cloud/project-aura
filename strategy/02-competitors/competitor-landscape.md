# Competitor Landscape — Ken Research Global Digital Experience Map

**Scope:** 20 competitors across 3 tiers  
**Primary lens:** Digital experience, UI/UX, and design — not report content quality  
**Last updated:** April 2026  
**Prepared for:** Ken Research internal strategy  

---

## How to Read This Document

This landscape map is organized around the experience layer — how each competitor presents itself digitally, how buyers discover and evaluate their products, and where the UX gaps are. Report content quality, methodological rigor, and analyst depth are secondary here. The digital experience IS the competitive battleground, and this document treats it as such.

Profile files with full detail live in `/profiles/`. The UX audit scorecard lives in `/ux-audits/digital-experience-scorecard.md`.

## Tier Definitions

### Tier A — Direct Syndicated-Report Competitors
These firms compete with Ken Research on the same buyer queries: "AI market size," "EV battery CAGR," "[sector] industry analysis." They publish syndicated reports in overlapping verticals, often at similar price points. This is the day-to-day competitive battleground.

### Tier B — Premium / Consulting-Flavored Competitors
These firms are larger, more expensive, and more enterprise-contract-oriented. They don't compete directly on $3K–$5K syndicated reports but they set buyer expectations for what "premium research" looks like. Gartner in particular is the experience north star — not a direct competitor but the UX standard the category is judged against.

### Tier C — Experience-Led / Data-Platform Competitors
These firms are building data products, not report catalogs. They represent where the category is going. Their UX is meaningfully better than Tier A because they invested in product design rather than sales-catalog infrastructure. They are the most important reference points for Ken Research's future experience layer.

## Tier A Deep-Dive: Key Patterns

### What All Tier A Players Share
1. **No pricing transparency** (except Research and Markets, Fortune Business Insights partially, Statista fully).
2. **Inquiry-first lead capture** — phone number, form fill, analyst contact required before seeing pricing.
3. **Taxonomy-based catalog navigation** — 10,000–30,000 reports navigable only through drilling hierarchical menus.
4. **Generic visual identity** — navy/blue corporate palette, stock photography, logo carousels.
5. **No meaningful data platform or dashboard** — report delivery is primarily PDF/PPT/Excel.
6. **Indian operations with Western registration** — majority are Pune/Hyderabad operations with US/UK registered addresses.
7. **Multiple certification badges** (ESOMAR, ISO, Great Place to Work) as primary trust signals.

### What Differentiates Within Tier A
- **Research and Markets** leads on pricing transparency and e-commerce checkout model.
- **Mordor Intelligence** leads on individual report page UX (chart previews, detailed TOC).
- **MarketsandMarkets** leads on platform ambition (KnowledgeStore, GrowthIQ) though execution is hidden behind demo walls.
- **Grand View Research** leads on product architecture naming (Horizon, Brainshare, Signal).
- **Fortune Business Insights** leads on CTA structure and case study social proof.
- **Statista** is a category apart — it is objectively the best UX in Tier A (and competes in Tier C simultaneously).
- **Technavio and Allied Market Research** trail the field on design and digital experience.

### The Tier A UX Gap Nobody Is Filling
No Tier A player (except Statista) has built a self-serve discovery experience that lets a buyer:
1. Search naturally ("What does the AI chip market look like in Southeast Asia by 2028?")
2. Preview meaningful data before committing to a sample request
3. See pricing clearly before raising their hand
4. Add a report to a cart and check out without speaking to an analyst

This is not a research product gap — it is a pure UX/commerce gap. Every Tier A competitor's website still behaves like a 2012 B2B lead-generation funnel rather than a 2026 research product experience.

## Tier B Deep-Dive: Key Patterns

### What Tier B Players Share
1. **Role-based or advisory-framed content** — research positioned as decision support, not data delivery.
2. **Enterprise contract model** — no self-serve pricing; subscription-only with sales handoff.
3. **Higher design investment** — better visual design than Tier A overall; more budget for brand.
4. **Proprietary frameworks** — Gartner Magic Quadrant, Forrester Wave, Frost Radar — each has a named analytical format with visual brand equity.
5. **Events businesses** — research + conferences creates two-sided revenue and relationship building.

### What Differentiates Within Tier B
- **Gartner** is the category's design standard; role-based IA and Peer Insights open platform set the bar.
- **Forrester** differentiates on brand voice ("Be Bold") and the Forrester Wave visual format.
- **GlobalData** differentiates on AI (Ava) and six-workflow platform architecture; publicly traded with disclosed financials.
- **IDC** differentiates on technology-sector depth, Tracker products, and "Analyst Firm of the Year" institutional credibility.
- **Frost & Sullivan** differentiates on Frost Radar™ and the awards/recognition business model that creates vendor marketing revenue.

## Tier C Deep-Dive: Key Patterns

### What Tier C Players Share
1. **Platform-first UX thinking** — products are designed around buyer workflows, not production catalogs.
2. **Freemium or free tier** — all four have some form of free access (CB Insights 10-day trial, PitchBook trial, Tracxn free signup, Crunchbase free tier).
3. **Modern visual design** — closer to SaaS product design than research firm design.
4. **AI-native features** — ChatCBI, predictive signals (CB Insights), PitchBook predictions, Crunchbase predictions, Tracxn deal flow — AI is embedded, not bolted on.
5. **Natural language search** — at least partial NL query capability in all four.
6. **Startup/private market focus** — all four are oriented around company intelligence rather than market sizing. This is the key overlap gap with traditional research.

### The Tier C Opportunity for Ken Research
Tier C players have the best UX but don't produce the industry-specific market sizing and CAGR research that Tier A buyers need. **Nobody has combined Tier C UX sophistication with Tier A research depth.** This is the most significant white space in the entire competitive landscape.

## Cross-Tier Meta-Observations

### 1. The Research Firm UX / Product UX Gap Is Wide and Growing
The gap between how research firms design their digital experiences (Tier A: transactional, form-heavy, opaque) and how data product companies design their experiences (Tier C: search-first, preview-forward, self-serve) is enormous and growing. Buyers who are used to Crunchbase's homepage will find Mordor Intelligence's catalog experience primitive.

### 2. AI Is Being Used As a Marketing Claim More Than a Product Reality
Every firm claims AI capabilities in 2026. The genuinely AI-native products (CB Insights ChatCBI, Euromonitor Passport AI, GlobalData Ava) are meaningfully better than competitors who have added "AI" to their homepage without changing the product. Buyers will increasingly be able to distinguish real AI integration from marketing claims.

### 3. Pricing Transparency Is the Single Biggest UX Opportunity in Tier A
Only Statista and Research and Markets show pricing publicly. Every other Tier A competitor forces buyers into a lead-capture funnel before they can evaluate cost. In a world where B2B buyers do 70%+ of their research before contacting a vendor, hiding pricing is a significant conversion and trust cost.

### 4. The "Platform" Claim Is Vastly Overused
MarketsandMarkets, Grand View Research, IMARC, and Mordor all claim platform products. None of them show these platforms publicly. Hiding a "platform" behind a demo wall is not a platform strategy — it's a sales motion using the word "platform." The actual platform companies (Statista, Euromonitor Passport, CB Insights, PitchBook) show their products, let users try them, and have active user bases measured in thousands or tens-of-thousands.

### 5. Design Investment Correlates Loosely But Not Perfectly with Revenue
- CB Insights (~$80M) has better design than MarketsandMarkets (~$315M).
- Crunchbase (~$36M) has better homepage UX than most of Tier B.
- Gartner ($6.5B) has the best overall design.
- Allied Market Research (~$24M) has arguably the worst.
- The pattern suggests design investment is a strategic choice, not purely a function of budget. Smaller firms that prioritize design can punch well above their revenue weight in experience quality.

### 6. Mobile Is Universally Neglected in Tier A
No Tier A competitor has invested meaningfully in mobile UX beyond "responsive web design." For a category whose buyers increasingly include startup analysts, investment professionals, and strategy consultants who work across devices, this is a structural experience gap.

### 7. Free Content as Brand Distribution Is Underused
Statista's embeddable charts generate millions of free brand impressions. CB Insights' free editorial research drives significant organic traffic. IDC's Tracker press releases are cited in every technology industry article. But most Tier A players produce little free, shareable content — they gate everything behind inquiry forms, leaving no free sample of the intelligence quality.
